\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether the command surface of a closed Android--\ros{} service robot can be recovered
without the manufacturer's help, and what evidence that recovery demands. The seven-phase,
triangulation-based pipeline produced a working surface on a commercial reception platform and
carried an edge deployment that replaces the vendor cloud for teleoperation, navigation, speech
and visitor interaction. The vendor application proved a dependable source of protocol names and
of the preconditions nobody documents (H1); the exposed broker proved incapable of carrying
commands at all (H2). Above everything else, the gap between transport and execution (H4)
governed the exercise: the addressing scheme we expected to matter (H3) explained none of our
outcomes, in the field or on the bench, while the precondition state explained all of them.
Establishing that took correcting our own measurement twice, which is the clearest evidence we
can offer for H4 --- on a recovered interface, the instrument is as easily fooled as the
integrator.

Next steps are a second manufacturer, a measured task comparison in place of our capability
table, and a language-model planner grounded in the primitives we validated rather than left to
rediscover the interface for itself.
